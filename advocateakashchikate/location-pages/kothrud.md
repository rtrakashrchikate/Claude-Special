## 498A / BNS 85 in Kothrud: What You Need to Know

False and exaggerated dowry harassment complaints are a painful reality in Kothrud and surrounding areas. Courts have acknowledged this — the Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasised the need for a pre-arrest inquiry — but an FIR, once filed, triggers a serious process that can affect your job, reputation, and freedom.

If you or your family have been named in a 498A / BNS 85 complaint, here is what matters most:

- **Act before arrest** — anticipatory bail applications must be filed promptly; waiting gives the police more time to build a case and reduces your options
- **Do not make statements** — avoid statements to police, WhatsApp groups, or relatives that can be produced in court as admissions
- **Gather evidence early** — call records, messages, financial records, and witness statements can all support your defence but must be preserved immediately
- **Counter-narrative from day one** — the investigation stage shapes the chargesheet; your lawyer's involvement early influences how the case is built

We also assist women in Kothrud who are facing genuine domestic violence and need urgent protection under the DV Act or criminal proceedings under 498A.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My wife has filed a false 498A case. What are my immediate options?**
A: Your first priority is anticipatory bail — file immediately, before police make an arrest, since 498A can be a cognisable and non-bailable offence in which police have power to arrest without a warrant. We simultaneously begin collecting evidence to counter the allegations, examine the complaint for false or exaggerated claims, and in appropriate cases file for FIR quashing at the Bombay High Court. Courts today take a calibrated approach to 498A — a well-documented defence at the investigation stage significantly improves outcomes at trial. Early engagement with a lawyer is the single most important step you can take.

**Q: My husband is abusive. What legal protection can I get quickly?**
A: The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 allows a Magistrate to grant a Protection Order (preventing the abuser from contacting or approaching you), a Residence Order (securing your right to stay in the shared home), and interim maintenance — and these orders can be obtained quickly, sometimes within days in urgent situations. In parallel, if the abuse involves physical harm or criminal conduct, an FIR under relevant BNS provisions (physical assault, criminal intimidation) may also be appropriate. We assess your situation confidentially and advise on the fastest and most effective legal route based on your specific facts.
