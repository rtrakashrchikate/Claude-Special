## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I get anticipatory bail if I think a false FIR will be filed against me?**
A: Yes — anticipatory bail is specifically designed for this situation. Under Section 438 of the CrPC (now BNSS), you can apply for anticipatory bail as soon as there is a reasonable apprehension of arrest, even before any FIR is registered. We assess your specific facts, identify the appropriate court (Sessions Court or Bombay High Court depending on the offence), and file the application promptly. If granted, you are protected from arrest for the duration specified — and in many cases the court's interim order alone prompts a resolution. The earlier you act, the wider your options.

**Q: A business partner has filed a cheating case against me over a failed deal. Is this a real criminal case?**
A: Cheating (BNS S.316 / IPC S.420) complaints arising from business disputes are extremely common, but courts look carefully at whether a genuine criminal intent existed at the time of the transaction — a breach of contract alone is not cheating. We examine the FIR, assess the strength of the criminal allegation versus a civil dispute, and where the complaint lacks criminal ingredients, apply for FIR quashing at the Bombay High Court. Simultaneously, bail or anticipatory bail is obtained to ensure you are not arrested while the matter is pending.
