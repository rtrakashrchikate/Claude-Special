## Road Accident Criminal Liability on the Expressway

Wakad's location near the Mumbai-Pune Expressway means that serious road accidents — and the criminal cases that follow — are a significant local concern. What most people do not realise is that a road accident involving death or grievous injuries can trigger criminal charges regardless of who was at fault:

- **Culpable homicide / rash and negligent driving** — applicable when a fatality results from negligent or rash driving; not limited to drunk driving situations
- **Motor Vehicles Act offences** — overspeeding, lane violations, and vehicle defects can all be used to establish criminal negligence
- **Insurance and FIR interaction** — how the FIR is worded directly affects your insurance claim; errors in the FIR can harm both the criminal case and the civil/insurance proceedings
- **Immediate arrest** — in serious accident cases, police often arrest at the scene; having a lawyer on call for bail applications at short notice is essential

If you or a family member has been involved in a serious road accident near Wakad or the Expressway, we advise you to contact a criminal lawyer before making any statement to police.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I received a notice from the cyber cell in Wakad. Do I need a lawyer?**
A: Yes — and you need one before you respond, not after. A cyber cell notice is a signal that police are actively investigating a complaint in which you are named. Anything you say in response — in writing or verbally — forms part of the police record and can shape the direction of the investigation. A lawyer reviews the notice, assesses the underlying complaint, advises you on what to say (and critically, what not to say), and determines whether anticipatory bail or FIR quashing is appropriate given your situation. Many cyber cell matters can be resolved at the investigation stage with proper legal intervention; once a chargesheet is filed, your options narrow considerably.

**Q: I was involved in a road accident on the Expressway. Can I face criminal charges?**
A: Yes. If the accident caused death or grievous bodily harm, police can register a case of culpable homicide not amounting to murder (BNS S.106 / IPC S.304A) or rash and negligent driving, which carries imprisonment. The FIR is often registered at the scene before a full investigation, which means the initial framing of the case matters enormously. We intervene early — reviewing the FIR, challenging incorrect or exaggerated facts, obtaining bail, and building a defence that accounts for road conditions, vehicle evidence, and witness statements. Acting quickly after the accident significantly improves your legal position.
