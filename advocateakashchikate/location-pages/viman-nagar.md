## Services for Viman Nagar Clients

### Bail with Travel Protections
For NRIs and frequent travellers, standard bail conditions — surrender of passport, no-travel restrictions — can be professionally devastating. We apply for bail conditions that explicitly protect travel rights, or file applications to modify existing conditions that are overly restrictive.

### NRI Matrimonial Criminal Matters
FIRs in 498A/BNS 85, DV Act proceedings, and matrimonial criminal cases involving NRIs — whether the NRI is in India or abroad — require a lawyer who understands both the criminal law and the practical complications of overseas parties, power of attorney arrangements, and cross-border evidence.

### Passport Impoundment Prevention & Reversal
Passport impoundment can occur as a bail condition, a court order, or a police action during investigation. We move to prevent impoundment before it happens, and where it has already occurred, file applications to restore the passport with appropriate conditions — urgent applications are possible where professional travel is imminent.

### FIR Quashing
Where a criminal complaint is false, motivated, or arises from a family or business dispute, we apply to quash the FIR at the Bombay High Court. For NRI clients abroad, this can be done with a power of attorney — you do not always need to be physically present in India to initiate the quashing process.

### Cybercrime Defence
Viman Nagar's tech-savvy and internationally connected population faces exposure to cybercrime complaints — online fraud allegations, digital harassment, identity theft, and social media FIRs. We defend individuals at the cyber cell level and in court.

### Anticipatory Bail for Overseas NRIs
If you are an NRI and fear arrest on return to India, anticipatory bail can be obtained in your absence through a lawyer with a power of attorney. This protects you from arrest the moment you land — and we can coordinate the application to align with your travel dates.

## NRI Criminal Exposure: What Viman Nagar Families Face

Living near the airport or maintaining strong overseas ties does not insulate you from Indian criminal law — in fact, it can increase your exposure:

- **FIRs filed while you are abroad** — relatives or estranged spouses file complaints knowing you cannot respond quickly; by the time you learn of it, a non-bailable warrant may already have been issued
- **498A / DV Act as leverage** — matrimonial criminal complaints are a common tool in NRI separation and divorce disputes; the threat of arrest on return to India is used as negotiating pressure
- **Property FIRs** — disputes over ancestral property or investments in Pune can escalate into criminal complaints of cheating, forgery, or land grabbing, with complaints filed against NRIs who are overseas
- **Lookout circulars** — in serious cases, police can request a Lookout Circular (LOC) preventing you from leaving India or, in some scenarios, flagging your entry; early legal intervention can prevent or challenge an LOC

If you are an NRI with concerns about any of the above, we can advise you remotely and act through a power of attorney where your physical presence is not required.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My passport was impounded as part of bail conditions. Can this be modified?**
A: Yes — bail conditions, including passport surrender, are not permanent and can be modified by the court that imposed them. We file an application to modify the bail conditions, presenting reasons why travel is necessary (professional obligations, medical treatment abroad, business commitments) and offering alternative safeguards if needed — such as a surety undertaking or regular reporting. Courts are generally open to modification where genuine need is demonstrated and the applicant has complied with other conditions. The application can often be heard on short notice where travel is time-sensitive. Do not cancel professional travel without first consulting a lawyer — a modification application filed promptly is far better than violating the condition or abandoning your trip.

**Q: I'm an NRI. My relatives filed an FIR against me while I was abroad. Do I need to return to India?**
A: Not necessarily — and returning without legal protection in place can be a serious mistake. If a non-bailable warrant has been issued or the offence is cognisable, you could be arrested at the airport. The first step is for a lawyer to verify the FIR and determine what orders, if any, exist against you. Depending on the situation, anticipatory bail can be obtained before you return to India, using a power of attorney — so that when you land, you are legally protected from arrest. In many NRI property and matrimonial FIRs, quashing at the Bombay High Court is also available, which would render the FIR void. We handle both tracks and advise you on the safest sequence before you book your ticket.
