## Helping Families of Undertrials and Convicted Prisoners

Families dealing with Yerawada jail often confuse the terms "undertrial" and "convicted prisoner" — and the distinction matters a great deal legally.

An **undertrial** is a person who has been arrested and is in custody while their case is still being heard — they have not been convicted of anything. A **convicted prisoner** has been found guilty after trial and is serving a sentence. The legal options available depend entirely on which category your family member falls into.

For undertrials, the main remedies are **bail** (a court order releasing the person on conditions like surety and appearance at hearings) and **default bail** under **Section 479 of BNSS** (the new Code of Criminal Procedure). Section 479 gives an undertrial the right to bail once they have spent half the maximum sentence for the offence in custody — for example, if the maximum sentence is 10 years, bail is available as of right after 5 years. First-time offenders get the benefit at one-third of the maximum sentence. This right is often not raised because families do not know it exists. We track these timelines for every client in custody.

For convicted prisoners, the remedies are different. A **criminal appeal** challenges the conviction or sentence before a higher court. An **appeal against sentence** alone can be filed if the conviction is accepted but the punishment is disputed. **Parole** and **furlough** are forms of temporary release granted by the prison administration (not courts) under Maharashtra rules — parole for emergencies like family illness or death, furlough as a periodic entitlement for good conduct. Each has its own eligibility criteria and paperwork.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My family member has been in Yerawada jail for 6 months as an undertrial. Can we get bail?**
A: Yes, and there are two routes. First, if bail was refused or not yet applied for, we can move a fresh bail application at the Sessions Court, or appeal a refusal to the Bombay High Court. Bail decisions look at the seriousness of the offence, criminal history, likelihood of absconding, and risk of tampering with witnesses. Second — and many families do not know this — Section 479 of BNSS gives undertrials a right to bail once they have served a set proportion of the maximum sentence for the offence. First-time offenders can claim this right at one-third of the maximum sentence. At six months, this right may already have arisen depending on the charges. Share the case details with us and we will assess both options immediately.

**Q: My relative was convicted. Can the conviction be challenged?**
A: Yes. A conviction by a Sessions Court can be appealed before the Bombay High Court. The appeal is not a re-trial — the High Court examines the trial record to determine whether the lower court made errors in appreciating evidence, applied the law incorrectly, or violated the accused's procedural rights. Sentence can also be appealed separately if the conviction itself is not in dispute. There are time limits for filing appeals, so the earlier you act after conviction, the better. We review the trial judgment and charge-sheet, identify the strongest grounds, and advise you honestly on the realistic prospects before filing.

**Q: How does parole work for prisoners in Yerawada?**
A: Parole in Maharashtra is governed by the Bombay Furlough and Parole Rules and is granted by the prison administration — not by a court. Regular parole is available for purposes like attending a family emergency (serious illness, death of a close relative), attending to urgent property or business matters, or other compelling reasons. The prisoner must have served a minimum period and must have a clean conduct record in jail. The application goes through the jail superintendent to the Inspector General of Prisons. Furlough is a separate entitlement — a periodic break from custody available as a reward for good conduct, typically available after serving a specified period. We prepare and submit both types of applications, follow up with the authority, and if the application is wrongly rejected, we can approach the courts for relief.
