## Divorce in Pune — What Warje Residents Ask Most Often

Divorce in India comes in two forms, and the path through the courts is very different depending on which applies to your situation.

**Mutual consent divorce** is available when both spouses agree that the marriage has broken down and agree on the main terms — property, maintenance, and children. Under Hindu Marriage Act Section 13B, the couple files a joint petition, waits six months (a "cooling-off" period), and then appears for a second hearing. Courts can waive the six-month period if the circumstances justify it. From filing to decree, a mutual consent divorce with a waiver can complete in as little as three to four months. This is by far the faster route.

**Contested divorce** — where one spouse opposes the divorce or disputes its terms — takes considerably longer. Grounds for contested divorce include cruelty, desertion, adultery, and conversion. Trials in contested divorce can run for several years, though courts regularly grant **interim orders** early in the proceedings. These interim orders can cover monthly maintenance for the spouse or children, temporary custody and visitation arrangements, and residence rights — so important protections are available while the full case works its way through the court.

**Children and property during proceedings.** A common concern is what happens to children and jointly held property while the divorce is pending. For children, a parent can apply for interim custody at any point. Courts will also make arrangements for the other parent to meet the children. For property, courts can pass orders preventing either party from disposing of jointly held assets during the case. Taking early legal advice matters here — the first few months often determine which party is better positioned on custody and property when the final order comes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the fastest way to file for divorce in Pune?**
A: Mutual consent divorce is the fastest route. Both spouses file a joint petition in family court agreeing on divorce, maintenance, property division, and children. There is a mandatory six-month waiting period between the first and second motion, but courts can waive this if the parties have already lived separately for a year or more and the marriage is clearly over. With a waiver, the full process from filing to decree can take three to four months. If both parties are cooperative and the terms are settled before filing, the process is relatively straightforward. Contested divorce — where one spouse opposes or disputes terms — takes much longer, often a year or more, though interim orders for maintenance and custody can be obtained within weeks of filing.

**Q: Can I get child custody while the divorce case is going on?**
A: Yes. You do not have to wait for the divorce to be finalised to get a custody order. Either parent can apply for interim custody at any stage during the divorce proceedings, and the family court will decide the arrangement based on the child's best interest — taking into account the child's age, the care each parent provides, stability, and the child's own preferences where the child is old enough to express them. The court will also put in place a visitation schedule for the parent who does not have interim custody. If the situation is urgent — for example, if the child is being kept away from you without reason — courts can act quickly. We have handled interim custody matters for clients in Warje and across Pune, and can advise you on the strength of your application and what documents you need to prepare.
