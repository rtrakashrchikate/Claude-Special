# Divorce in Pune: Your Rights, Timeline & What to Expect

*By Advocate Akash R. Chikate | Family Law | Pune Family Court*

---

## Before Anything Else — Know This

Divorce is a legal process, and like any legal process, the outcome depends heavily on the decisions you make early. This guide gives you the information to make those decisions wisely.

Everything here is about your rights — as a husband, as a wife, as a parent. The law protects everyone. Knowing your rights means you can enforce them.

---

## Your Rights in a Divorce

### Right to Maintenance
A financially dependent spouse has the right to **interim maintenance** — payable from the date of the application, not the date of final settlement. Courts grant interim maintenance at the very first hearing in many cases.

**What is considered:** husband's income, lifestyle, wife's income (or lack of it), expenses for children.

### Right to Residence
Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, a wife has the right to continue residing in the shared household — even if she doesn't own it — unless a court specifically orders otherwise.

### Right to Custody (Interim)
Both parents have an equal right to apply for custody of children. Interim custody is available immediately upon filing. Courts decide based on the best interests of the child.

### Right to Property
In matrimonial disputes, courts can issue injunctions preventing your spouse from selling, transferring, or mortgaging shared assets until the case is decided.

### Right to a Free Legal Aid Lawyer
If you cannot afford a lawyer, you have the right to free legal representation from the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA), Pune.

## Types of Divorce — Which Applies to You?

| Type | Who Applies | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Mutual Consent (HMA S.13B) | Both spouses agree | 6–18 months |
| Contested Divorce | One spouse files | 3–7 years |
| Divorce by Mutual Agreement (SMA) | Civil / inter-religion marriage | 6–18 months |
| Divorce under Muslim Law | Muslim couples | Varies |

**Fastest route:** Mutual consent — both parties agree on divorce, custody, maintenance, and property. Two court hearings and you're done.

## The Mutual Consent Divorce Process (Step-by-Step)

1. **Decide the terms** — custody, maintenance, property division (crucial to agree before filing)
2. **File joint petition** at Pune Family Court, Shivajinagar
3. **First motion hearing** — both spouses appear, statements recorded
4. **6-month cooling-off period** — mandatory waiting period (can be waived in some cases)
5. **Second motion hearing** — both spouses confirm decision to divorce
6. **Divorce decree** — marriage legally dissolved

**Can the 6 months be waived?** Yes — the Supreme Court in *Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur* held that courts can waive the 6-month period if the marriage has irretrievably broken down and both parties are firm in their decision.

---

## The Contested Divorce Process

1. Petition filed by one spouse (the petitioner) at Family Court
2. Served on the other spouse (the respondent)
3. Respondent files a written statement
4. **Mandatory mediation** at the Mediation Centre, Shivajinagar
5. If mediation fails → evidence stage (witnesses, documents)
6. Arguments
7. Judgment

**Interim orders available throughout:**
- Interim maintenance (filed at any stage)
- Interim custody
- Injunction against property disposal

## Child Custody — What You Need to Know

**Types of custody:**
- **Physical custody** — where the child lives day-to-day
- **Legal custody** — right to make decisions about education, health, etc.
- **Joint custody** — both parents share time and/or decisions
- **Sole custody** — one parent has primary custody; the other has visitation rights

**What courts consider:**
- Child's age and gender (very young children often with mother)
- Child's own preference (if old enough to form a view)
- Which parent has been the primary caregiver
- Which parent can provide stability, schooling, and emotional support
- Each parent's behaviour during the marriage (allegations of cruelty, abuse)

**Grandparents' rights:** Grandparents can apply for visitation rights — and sometimes guardianship if both parents are unable to care for the child.

## Maintenance — The Numbers

There is no fixed formula. Courts consider:

- Husband's income (salary slips, ITR, lifestyle are all evidence)
- Wife's income or earning capacity
- Standard of living during marriage
- Children's needs

**Typical ranges (Pune courts):** Interim maintenance varies widely — from ₹5,000/month in lower-income cases to ₹50,000+/month for high-income couples. Permanent alimony is typically a lump sum based on earning capacity and need.

## Key Questions + Answers

**Q: Can I file for divorce if my spouse is abroad?**
A: Yes — service of notice by post to the last known address, or via the Indian embassy/consulate in the relevant country. NRI divorce has additional procedural steps but is absolutely possible.

**Q: Can divorce proceedings be kept private?**
A: Family Court proceedings are not open to the general public. The judgment is on record, but day-to-day hearings are relatively private.

**Q: What if my spouse doesn't respond to the divorce petition?**
A: After proper service, the court can proceed ex-parte (without the respondent's participation) if they consistently fail to appear.

**Q: Does the wife always get the matrimonial home?**
A: No — property division depends on ownership, financial contribution, and specific facts. Courts don't automatically give the home to either party.

**Q: I was married under the Special Marriage Act. Does Hindu Marriage Act apply?**
A: No — SMA has its own divorce provisions. The procedure is similar but the law differs. We advise on the specific act applicable to your marriage.
