# What to Do in the First 24 Hours After an FIR Is Filed Against You

*A plain-language action guide by Advocate Akash R. Chikate, Criminal Defence Attorney, Pune*

---

## Why the First 24 Hours Matter

An FIR (First Information Report) sets a criminal process in motion. Once filed, police have the authority to investigate and arrest without a court order (in cognisable offences). What you do — and don't do — in the first 24 hours can be the difference between going home tonight and spending weeks in custody.

---

## HOUR 1–3: Immediate Actions

### ✅ Get a copy of the FIR
You have a legal right to a free copy of the FIR under Section 173 BNSS. Ask the police station directly or access via CopConnect/state police portal.

**What to look for in the FIR:**
- Sections charged (bailable vs. non-bailable?)
- Specific allegations (are they true? false? exaggerated?)
- Names of other accused
- Name of complainant

### ✅ Call a criminal lawyer
Not tomorrow. Now. Even a 10-minute call with a lawyer tells you whether you're at risk of arrest and what your immediate options are.

**If you can't reach a lawyer yet:** Do not go to the police station on your own.

### ✅ Do NOT speak to anyone about the case
Not to friends. Not to family. Not on WhatsApp. Phone calls are recorded. Messages are evidence. The more you say, the more risk you create.

### ❌ Do NOT contact the complainant
Reaching out to the complainant — even to explain or apologise — is treated as witness tampering or an attempt to compound without court permission. Don't do it.

### ❌ Do NOT delete anything
Messages, photos, emails, files — preserve everything exactly as it is. Deleting data after an FIR is evidence tampering. Even if the content is embarrassing, keep it.

---

## HOUR 3–12: Legal Action

### ✅ Assess bail risk immediately
Your lawyer will tell you:
- Is the FIR for a bailable or non-bailable offence?
- Is arrest likely in the next 24–48 hours?
- Should you file for anticipatory bail immediately?

**Anticipatory bail** — file this if arrest is likely for a non-bailable offence. It protects you before any arrest happens. Act before the police act.

### ✅ Gather documents your lawyer will need
- Your address proof (Aadhaar, passport, utility bill)
- Identity proof
- Employment proof (job letter, pay slip) — shows roots in Pune
- Names and addresses of family members who can stand surety
- Any documents relevant to the allegations (contracts, messages, bank records)

### ✅ Brief your lawyer on the full facts
Tell your lawyer the true and complete facts — including anything that seems bad. Lawyers who know the weak points of a case protect you far better than those who are kept in the dark.

---

## HOUR 12–24: Investigation & Evidence Preservation

### ✅ Preserve your digital footprint
Take screenshots of:
- WhatsApp conversations with the complainant
- Any evidence contradicting the allegations
- Your location history / digital receipts from relevant dates
- Emails, contracts, payment records

Back up everything to a secure cloud account.

### ✅ Identify potential witnesses
Who saw what? Who knows the truth? Your lawyer may need witnesses at trial or to support bail. Identify them now while memories are fresh.

### ✅ Stop social media activity
Do not post, comment, or share anything about the case or related topics. Courts increasingly look at social media. Nothing you post helps you; it can only hurt.

---

## THE GOLDEN RULES

1. **Say nothing without your lawyer** — to police, complainant, or anyone
2. **Delete nothing** — preserve all digital evidence
3. **Act fast on bail** — the window for anticipatory bail is short
4. **Tell your lawyer everything** — the full truth, always
5. **Don't make deals informally** — any settlement must go through the court
