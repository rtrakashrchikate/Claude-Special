# Cybercrime FIR in Pune: Your 7-Day Action Plan

*By Advocate Akash R. Chikate | IT Act & Cybercrime Defence | Pune*

---

## DAY 1 — Don't Panic. Don't Act Without a Lawyer.

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Do not delete messages, files, or accounts
- ❌ Do not call, message, or confront the complainant
- ❌ Do not post anything on social media
- ❌ Do not go to the police station or cyber cell alone
- ❌ Do not pay anyone to "settle" the matter informally

**What to do:**
- ✅ Get a copy of the FIR or cyber cell notice
- ✅ Screenshot all relevant messages and documents
- ✅ Call a cybercrime defence lawyer immediately

---

## DAY 1–2 — Understand What You're Facing

Your lawyer will help you answer these questions:

**Is it a Notice or an FIR?**
- A **cyber cell notice** (Section 160/41A BNSS) means police want to talk to you. You don't have to appear without a lawyer.
- An **FIR** means a case is registered. Arrest risk is real.

**What section are you charged under?**
| Section | Offence | Bailable? |
|---|---|---|
| IT Act S.66 | Hacking / unauthorised access | Yes (up to 3 years) |
| IT Act S.66C | Identity theft | Yes |
| IT Act S.66D | Cheating by impersonation | Yes |
| BNS S.316 + IT Act | Online fraud | Depends on amount |
| NDPS-related cyber | Drug trafficking via internet | No (Section 37) |

**Are you a "money mule"?**
If your bank account was used to receive/transfer fraud proceeds without your knowledge — this is a common situation and there is a strong defence. Tell your lawyer everything.

---

## DAY 2–3 — Legal Action: Anticipatory Bail

If you've been named as accused in a cybercrime FIR and arrest is possible:

**File for anticipatory bail immediately.**

Your lawyer will file at the Sessions Court or Bombay High Court. Interim protection (stay of arrest) is commonly granted at the first hearing — within 24–48 hours of filing.

You cannot get anticipatory bail after you're arrested. Act before police act.

---

## DAY 3–4 — Evidence Preservation

Work with your lawyer to preserve:

- [ ] All communications with the complainant (WhatsApp, email, SMS)
- [ ] Bank statements showing when transfers happened (and that you didn't initiate them if it's a mule case)
- [ ] Login/access logs if relevant (showing you didn't access the alleged system)
- [ ] Witness contact list — who can confirm you were elsewhere or uninvolved?
- [ ] Device seizure memo if police already took devices — confirm everything listed

---

## DAY 4–5 — Assess FIR Quashing Prospects

Not all cybercrime FIRs need to go to trial. Your lawyer will assess whether FIR quashing at the Bombay High Court is viable based on:

- Is the dispute essentially civil (payment not received, contract dispute re-labelled as fraud)?
- Are you falsely implicated (mule account, stolen identity)?
- Has the matter been or can it be settled?
- Are the FIR allegations too vague to constitute a specific offence?

If quashing is viable — file immediately. Interim stay of arrest at first hearing while the quashing petition is heard.

---

## DAY 5–7 — Prepare Your Response Strategy

Your lawyer will present you with your options:

**Option A — Cooperate with investigation (with safeguards)**
Appear before cyber cell with lawyer, give a prepared statement, and demonstrate innocence through documents. Works well for mule cases where you genuinely had no involvement.

**Option B — Challenge the case procedurally**
Challenge the jurisdiction, the FIR's cognisability, or the specific charges. Best where the FIR is weak on law.

**Option C — Settle with complainant + quash the FIR**
In fraud cases where a genuine settlement is possible, resolving the civil dispute often leads to FIR quashing. The complainant withdraws, High Court quashes.

**Option D — Defend at trial**
For cases that cannot be resolved early — full criminal trial defence, challenging digital evidence, cross-examining prosecution witnesses.

---

## Remember

You are innocent until proven guilty. The FIR is an allegation — not a conviction. With the right legal strategy in the first week, many cybercrime cases are resolved without trial.
