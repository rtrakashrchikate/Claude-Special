## Criminal Law Services

### Anticipatory Bail
If you have reason to believe an FIR will be filed or an arrest is imminent, anticipatory bail (Section 482 BNSS) protects you before it happens. We file at the Sessions Court or Bombay High Court and routinely secure interim protection at the first hearing.

**Act now if:**
- You've been called for police questioning
- A family dispute or business dispute is escalating
- Someone has threatened to file an FIR against you
- You're named in another accused's statement

### FIR Quashing at Bombay High Court
The Bombay High Court can quash an FIR under Section 528 BNSS where:
- No cognisable offence is disclosed on the face of the FIR
- The complaint is false, frivolous, or vexatious
- The dispute is essentially civil in nature
- A genuine settlement has been reached between parties
- Continued prosecution would be oppressive or an abuse of process

Once quashed, the FIR, charge sheet, and all pending proceedings are permanently set aside.

**Timeline:** We file within days of being retained. Interim stay of arrest is often granted at the first hearing.

### Criminal Appeals & Revision Petitions
Convicted by the Magistrate's Court or Sessions Court? You have a right of appeal. We file:

### Sessions Trial Defence

### NDPS Act Cases
Drug cases under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act carry stringent bail conditions under Section 37. We have experience building the threshold argument required for NDPS bail and providing full NDPS trial defence.

### POCSO Cases
POCSO (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act) cases are sensitive and carry severe consequences. We provide defence representation with strict attention to the evidentiary requirements and procedural safeguards — including bail applications where legally permissible.

### Cheque Bounce (NI Act S.138)
Cheque bounce cases are criminal — they can result in imprisonment of up to 2 years. We represent both complainants (creditors) and accused in NI Act S.138 proceedings, including trial, compounding, and appeals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How quickly can I get bail after arrest in Pune?**
A: For bailable offences — within hours at the police station. For non-bailable offences, a Magistrate hears the application within 24 hours of production. Sessions Court bail takes 3–5 days. HC bail takes 5–10 days in most matters. Emergency urgent listings are available in exceptional circumstances.

**Q: What is the difference between FIR quashing and acquittal?**
A: FIR quashing is a pre-trial remedy — it stops the case before it reaches full trial. Acquittal happens at the end of a trial when the court finds you not guilty. Quashing is faster (months vs. years) and leaves no criminal record.

**Q: Can a convicted person get bail during appeal?**
A: Yes — suspension of sentence pending appeal can be obtained from the appellate court. The convicted person is released on bail until the appeal is decided, which can take years. We file these applications immediately after conviction.
