## POST 3: Cybercrime FIR in Pune — What to Do in the First 24 Hours

**URL:** /blog/cybercrime-fir-pune-what-to-do/
**Title:** Cybercrime FIR in Pune: What You Must Do in the First 24 Hours (2026)
**Meta:** Received a cybercrime FIR or notice in Pune? Step-by-step guide for the first 24 hours — do's, don'ts, and when to call a lawyer. By Adv. Akash Chikate.

### Intro
A cybercrime FIR or cyber cell notice lands on your phone or at your door without warning. Most people make critical mistakes in the first 24 hours — mistakes that make their case dramatically harder to defend. This guide tells you exactly what to do and what to avoid.

### Section 1: Don't Do This (Common Mistakes)
- Do not call or WhatsApp the complainant
- Do not delete messages, files, emails, or app data — this is evidence tampering
- Do not give any statement to police without a lawyer
- Do not "explain yourself" on social media
- Do not send money to make the problem "go away" — this is often used as an admission

### Section 2: Do This Immediately
1. Note the case number and police station from the notice
2. Screenshot everything — the notice, any related messages
3. Preserve all digital devices exactly as they are
4. Call a criminal lawyer — the first 24 hours define the trajectory

### Section 3: Is It a Notice or an FIR? (Know the Difference)
- **Section 160 BNSS notice:** Witness summons — you must appear (usually). No right to remain silent as witness.
- **Accused notice:** You've been named as accused. You do NOT have to appear without a lawyer and can invoke your right against self-incrimination.
- **FIR:** A case has been registered. Bail risk is real.

### Section 4: When Is Anticipatory Bail Needed?
If you've been named as accused in a cybercrime FIR, file for anticipatory bail immediately — don't wait to see if police come for you.

### Section 5: The "Money Mule" Situation
If your bank account was used to route fraud proceeds without your knowledge, you are likely named as a mule. The defence: you had no knowledge of or participation in the fraud. This is a strong and commonly successful defence, but requires immediate legal action.

### Intro
Deciding to end a marriage is one of the hardest decisions you'll ever make. This guide explains the legal process in plain language — so you know what to expect before you start.

### Section 1: Types of Divorce in India
- Mutual consent (HMA S.13B) — fastest, both parties agree
- Contested divorce — one party files, other contests
- Divorce by mutual agreement under Special Marriage Act (for civil/inter-religion marriages)

### Section 2: Grounds for Divorce in India
Cruelty (most common), desertion (2 years), adultery, mental disorder, leprosy (removed), venereal disease, conversion, and irretrievable breakdown (being introduced by court practice).

### Section 3: The Mutual Consent Divorce Process
1. Joint petition filed at Pune Family Court
2. First motion hearing — statement recorded
3. 6-month cooling-off period (courts can waive if parties genuinely want divorce)
4. Second motion — final decree
Timeline: 6–18 months

### Section 4: The Contested Divorce Process
1. Petition filed, served on respondent
2. Written statement by respondent
3. Mediation (mandatory) — Family Court, Mediation Centre
4. Evidence stage — witnesses, documents
5. Arguments
6. Judgment
Timeline: 3–7 years (much shorter with mediation settlement)

### Section 5: Interim Orders During Divorce
- Interim maintenance — available at first hearing
- Interim custody — available urgently
- Injunction against property transfer
- Residence order

### Section 6: What About NRI Divorce?
One-sided NRI divorces (foreign decree without proper service) are not automatically valid in India. We navigate cross-jurisdiction divorce for NRIs.

## POST 5: NDPS Act in Pune — Bail, Defence, and What Actually Happens

**URL:** /blog/ndps-act-bail-defence-pune/
**Title:** NDPS Act in Pune: Bail, Section 37, and Your Defence Options (2026)
**Meta:** Arrested under NDPS Act in Pune? Section 37 bail explained, defence options, key judgments. Adv. Akash Chikate — Bombay HC. Free consultation.

### Intro
An NDPS arrest is terrifying — and the law makes bail extremely difficult. But "difficult" doesn't mean impossible. This is a no-jargon guide to NDPS cases in Pune.

### Section 1: What the NDPS Act Covers
Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. Covers cannabis, heroin, cocaine, methamphetamine, synthetic drugs, and prescription medications without valid prescription.

### Section 2: Quantities Matter Enormously
- **Small quantity** — lower punishment, regular bail
- **More than small but less than commercial** — intermediate, bail at Sessions level
- **Commercial quantity** — highest punishment, strictest bail criteria

For cannabis: small quantity = 1kg, commercial = 20kg+. For heroin: small = 5g, commercial = 250g+.

### Section 3: Section 37 — The Bail Threshold
Unlike ordinary bail, NDPS Section 37 requires the court to be satisfied:
1. There are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is NOT guilty
2. The accused is not likely to commit any offence while on bail

This is a higher bar — but achievable with the right application.

**What makes a strong NDPS bail application:**
- First-time offender with no criminal record
- Small or intermediate quantity
- Accused was present but not a principal actor
- Medical condition requiring treatment outside prison
- Strong ties to Pune (family, employment, property)
- Time already spent in custody

### Section 4: Challenging the Evidence
NDPS cases often have procedural flaws:
- Recovery witnessed by "panch witnesses" who later turn hostile
- Improper sampling procedure (entire consignment not weighed in accused's presence)
- Custody gap — seized material tampered with before testing
- No independent witness for recovery
- Violation of Section 42/43 NDPS procedure (search & seizure requirements)

### Section 5: Key Bombay High Court Judgments
*Tofan Singh v. State of Tamil Nadu* — statements to NCB officers not admissible (crucial for confession-based cases). *State v. Mukesh* — strict interpretation of Section 37. *Union of India v. Ram Samujh* — bail principles in commercial quantity cases.

## POST 6: Cheque Bounce Cases in Pune — Accused and Complainant Guide

**URL:** /blog/cheque-bounce-case-pune/
**Title:** Cheque Bounce Case in Pune (NI Act S.138): Complete Guide for 2026
**Meta:** Cheque bounce case in Pune — filed against you or need to file one? NI Act S.138 process, bail, compounding, defences. Adv. Akash Chikate. Free consultation.

### Intro
Cheque bounce (dishonour of cheque) under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act is one of the most common criminal cases in Pune's courts — and one of the most misunderstood. This guide covers both sides: what complainants need to do to succeed, and what accused persons can do to defend themselves.

### Section 1: What Triggers a Cheque Bounce Case
1. Cheque is dishonoured by bank ("insufficient funds", "account closed", "payment stopped")
2. Payee sends a legal demand notice within 30 days of dishonour
3. Drawer fails to pay within 15 days of receiving the notice
4. Payee files a complaint in the Magistrate's Court within 30 days of expiry of the 15-day period

**All four conditions must be met** — a complaint without proper notice is liable to be dismissed.

### Section 2: For Complainants — How to Win
- Send the demand notice by registered post AND email/WhatsApp (to establish receipt)
- Keep the envelope with postal acknowledgment
- File within the strict 30-day window after the notice period
- Produce the original cheque, bank memo, and notice at trial

### Section 3: For Accused — Defences Available
- **No legally enforceable debt** — the cheque was for a different purpose (gift, security, blank cheque misuse)
- **Notice not properly served** — if the notice didn't reach you, the 15-day period didn't start
- **Cheque issued without authority** — for company cheques
- **Limitation expired** — complaint filed after 30-day window
- **Compounding** — settle with the complainant and close the case (the most common resolution)

### Section 4: Punishment and Bail
Up to 2 years imprisonment or twice the cheque amount, or both. **Bail is available** — Magistrate Court bail is standard for NI Act S.138 cases.

### Section 5: Compounding — The Practical Resolution
NI Act S.138 is compoundable with the court's permission. The accused pays the cheque amount (and often additional compensation) and the case is closed. This is the standard resolution for most cheque bounce cases — pragmatic for both parties.

## POST 8: Anticipatory Bail in Pune — When to Apply and How

**URL:** /blog/anticipatory-bail-pune/
**Title:** Anticipatory Bail in Pune: When to Apply, How It Works & Timeline (2026)
**Meta:** Anticipatory bail in Pune — when you need it, how to apply, what conditions courts impose. Adv. Akash Chikate, Bombay HC. Free consultation.

### Intro
Anticipatory bail is one of the most powerful protective tools in Indian criminal law — and one of the least used, because most people don't know to apply until they're already arrested. This guide tells you when anticipatory bail is relevant, how it works, and what to do today if you think you need it.

### Section 1: What is Anticipatory Bail?
A bail granted in anticipation of arrest — before any FIR is filed or before any arrest happens. Section 482 BNSS (formerly S.438 CrPC). Granted by Sessions Court or Bombay High Court.

### Section 2: When Do You Need Anticipatory Bail?
- A matrimonial dispute is escalating and threats of FIR have been made
- You've been called for police questioning as a suspect
- A business dispute is turning criminal
- You've received a demand notice that implies a criminal complaint will follow
- You're aware of a statement made by another accused naming you
- A criminal case was earlier closed and you fear re-opening

**The golden rule:** Apply before it's too late. Once you're in custody, it's too late for anticipatory bail — only regular bail applies.

### Section 3: Conditions Typically Imposed
Courts grant anticipatory bail with conditions. Common conditions:
- Report to a specified police station weekly/fortnightly
- Surrender passport (for serious matters)
- Don't leave Pune/Maharashtra without permission
- Don't tamper with evidence or contact witnesses
- Make yourself available for investigation when required

These conditions can be modified by application once the initial protection is granted.

### Section 4: Duration of Anticipatory Bail
The Supreme Court in *Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)* held that anticipatory bail need not be limited in time — it can continue until the end of the trial. However, courts may impose a limited term initially with renewal.

### Section 5: Anticipatory Bail at Sessions Court vs High Court
- Sessions Court: faster first hearing (1–3 days), but jurisdiction limited to Sessions-level matters
- Bombay High Court: broader jurisdiction, higher chance of success in complex matters, slightly longer timelines but urgent listings available
- We assess which forum is best for your specific situation
