# How to File a Consumer Court Complaint in Pune (2026) — Step-by-Step

Most people who have been cheated by a builder, denied an insurance claim, or overcharged by a bank assume their only remedy is to write angry emails to customer service. They don't know that India has a dedicated consumer protection system — the Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (CDRC) — where you can sue a builder, an e-commerce platform, a hospital, or a bank and receive a legally enforceable order for refund, replacement, and real compensation for mental agony, at a fraction of the cost and time of civil court litigation.

**What this guide covers:**

- Who qualifies as a consumer and which entities can be sued
- Which court to file in, based on your claim amount (Pune-specific)
- The complete eight-step filing process at CDRC Pune
- What compensation you can realistically claim
- Builder cases — the biggest category in Pune
- Bank and insurance disputes
- Five practical FAQs

## Who Can File a Consumer Complaint — and Against Whom

A **consumer** under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is any person who buys goods or hires services for personal use — not for commercial resale or for someone else's manufacturing process. The definition is broader than most people realise.

**Covered by the Act:**

- Purchase of a flat or plot (goods and services combined)
- Hospital procedures, clinic fees, diagnostic tests
- Gym memberships, coaching institute fees, online courses
- Online purchases (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, Myntra)
- Airline tickets, hotel bookings, travel packages
- Gifts — if a product was gifted to you and is defective, you have standing

**You can file against:**

- Builders and developers
- Banks, NBFCs, and credit card companies
- Insurance companies (life, health, motor, property)
- Private hospitals and diagnostic centres
- Educational institutions (degree-granting bodies)
- Telecom operators and internet service providers
- E-commerce platforms and sellers
- Government services that charge a fee

**What is not covered:** Goods or services purchased purely for commercial resale, or used as inputs in a manufacturing or trading business. If you buy raw material for your factory or goods for your retail shop, you are not a "consumer" under the Act and must pursue other civil remedies.

## Which Consumer Court to File In — Pune

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 created a three-tier structure. The correct forum depends on the total value of your claim — goods/services value plus all compensation you are seeking.

| Forum | Claim Value | Location |
|---|---|---|
| District CDRC, Pune | Up to ₹50 lakhs | Shivajinagar, Pune |
| Maharashtra State Commission | ₹50 lakhs to ₹2 crore | Mumbai |
| National CDRC (NCDRC) | Above ₹2 crore | New Delhi |

Appeals run from District CDRC → State Commission → NCDRC → Supreme Court of India.

**Pune-specific advantage:** Under the Consumer Protection Act, you can file at a CDRC either where **you reside** or where the **opposite party is located** — your choice. Pune residents dealing with a Mumbai-registered builder or a Bengaluru-headquartered e-commerce company can file at the Pune District CDRC, saving time, travel, and cost.

The Pune District CDRC sits at the Civil Court complex, Shivajinagar — the same campus that houses the District and Sessions Court. Most individual consumer disputes in Pune fall within the ₹50 lakh limit and are filed here.

## Step-by-Step Filing Process at CDRC Pune

### Step 1 — Gather Your Evidence

Before filing anything, assemble every document that proves your transaction and the deficiency. This includes:

- Purchase agreement, allotment letter, or receipt
- Bank statements showing payment
- All correspondence (emails, letters, WhatsApp chats)
- Photographs or video of defects
- Any communication where the company acknowledged the problem or promised resolution
- Product warranties or policy documents

Consumer courts are documentary forums — the strength of your evidence determines your outcome at least as much as the law does.

### Step 2 — Send a Formal Grievance Notice to the Company

Before approaching the CDRC, write a formal complaint letter to the company's grievance officer or registered address. Set out the deficiency clearly and state what remedy you want (refund, repair, replacement, or compensation). This step is practically mandatory, gives the company a last chance to resolve, and creates a documented paper trail showing you acted in good faith. Allow 30 days for a response.

### Step 3 — Draft the Consumer Complaint

If no satisfactory response is received, proceed to draft the complaint. The document must cover three things:

1. **Facts:** what you bought, when, from whom, and what went wrong
2. **Deficiency in service or goods:** the legal basis for your claim
3. **Prayer (relief sought):** specific and quantified — refund amount, compensation, interest, litigation costs

The prayer clause is critical. An inadequately drafted prayer limits the compensation the court can award you.

### Step 4 — File at CDRC Pune with Court Fee

File the complaint at the CDRC registry, Shivajinagar, with self-attested copies of all supporting documents and a verification affidavit. Court fees are very modest:

- Claims up to ₹5 lakhs: ₹200
- ₹5 lakhs to ₹10 lakhs: ₹400
- ₹10 lakhs to ₹20 lakhs: ₹500
- ₹20 lakhs to ₹50 lakhs: ₹2,000

### Step 5 — Registration and Notice to Opposite Party

The CDRC registry checks the complaint for completeness and assigns a case number. The Commission then issues **notice to the opposite party**, who has 30 days to file a written response. The opposite party can request additional time in complex matters.

### Step 6 — Hearings and Evidence

After the written version is filed, both sides are heard. Evidence is typically filed by affidavit — you do not need to appear physically for every hearing, particularly in uncontested matters. Your lawyer files written arguments and the Commission decides.

### Step 7 — Order

The CDRC passes a reasoned order directing refund, replacement, or compensation — or dismissing the complaint. The order is legally enforceable. If the opposite party does not comply, execution proceedings can compel compliance, including attachment of assets.

**Typical timeline:**
- Straightforward uncontested matter: 3–6 months
- Contested builder or insurance dispute: 12–18 months

## What Compensation Can You Claim

Consumer courts are not limited to ordering a simple refund. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 empowers the CDRC to award a comprehensive package of relief.

**Full refund or replacement**, with **interest** running from the date of payment or default. Courts typically award interest at 9%–12% per annum — meaningful in builder disputes where payment was made years before possession.

**Compensation for mental agony and harassment** is a distinct and separately awarded head of damages. In builder delay cases, the Pune District CDRC and the Maharashtra State Commission have awarded ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakhs as compensation for harassment, depending on the duration and the builder's conduct. Hospital negligence and insurance rejection cases have attracted higher awards.

**Litigation costs** — reasonable lawyer's fees and travel expenses incurred attending hearings. Courts award these routinely.

**Punitive damages** in egregious cases — particularly where large companies have a pattern of ignoring consumer complaints. Courts have imposed punitive damages as a deterrent even where the actual financial loss was modest.

## Builder Cases — The Biggest Consumer Complaints in Pune

Pune has one of the highest concentrations of builder-buyer disputes in Maharashtra. Projects across Hinjewadi, Wakad, Baner, Hadapsar, Kharadi, Undri, and dozens of other fast-developing localities have generated thousands of CDRC complaints.

**Possession delay** is the most common complaint. If your builder has missed the possession date in your agreement, you are entitled to compensation for every month of delay. Consumer courts in Pune have awarded ₹5,000 to ₹20,000 per month of delay, plus interest at 9%–12% on all amounts paid.

**Construction defects** — seepage, wall cracks, substandard finishing, non-functional lifts, promised amenities not delivered — are the second most common complaint. The limitation period is two years from the date the defect was **discovered**, not the date of possession. Do not assume you are out of time.

**RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Authority) Maharashtra** is a parallel remedy that runs alongside consumer court. RERA is generally faster for possession claims and interest on delayed possession. Consumer court is better for mental agony compensation. The optimal strategy in complex builder disputes is to pursue both simultaneously — RERA for possession and interest, CDRC for compensation and litigation costs. We advise on the right combination at the first consultation.

Other builder complaints covered: carpet area shortfalls, amenity non-delivery, society formation delays, and non-provision of promised car parking.

## Bank and Insurance Complaints — Second Most Common

The second-largest category of consumer complaints in Pune involves banks and insurance companies — and this is an area where consumers regularly receive favourable orders because the deficiency is almost always clearly documented in bank statements and policy documents.

**Banking complaints handled by CDRC:**

- Unauthorised debit or withdrawal from your account
- Wrong charges, hidden fees, or penalty reversals refused
- EMI deducted despite a documented settlement or moratorium
- CIBIL score errors caused by the bank's erroneous reporting
- Credit card disputes and fraudulent transactions

**Insurance complaints handled by CDRC:**

- Claim outright rejected without adequate or lawful grounds
- Unreasonable delay in claim settlement
- Policy mis-sold (product described differently from actual terms)
- Health insurance claim partially repudiated

> **Practical tip:** Before approaching the CDRC for banking matters, file a complaint with the **RBI Banking Ombudsman**. For insurance, use the **IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal** first. These are free and faster for straightforward cases. Consumer court is the correct forum when you also want compensation for mental agony on top of the refund — the Ombudsman cannot award that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Do I need a lawyer to file a consumer complaint in Pune?

You do not strictly need a lawyer for small straightforward matters — the consumer court system was designed to be accessible to self-represented complainants. In practice, however, having a lawyer matters significantly in three situations: first, contested cases where the opposite party has in-house or retained legal teams (common with builders and insurance companies); second, complex cases involving large amounts where the drafting of the complaint and the prayer clause affects your compensation outcome; and third, cases close to the limitation deadline or involving jurisdictional questions. A free first consultation will tell you honestly whether you need legal assistance or whether you can file independently.

### Q: What is the time limit to file a consumer complaint?

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 prescribes a **two-year limitation period** from the date the cause of action arose. For a possession delay case, limitation runs from the committed possession date. For a defective product, from the date of purchase or the date you discovered the defect. For an insurance rejection, from the date of the rejection letter. Courts can condone delay if you show sufficient cause, but it is always safer to file within two years. If you are approaching the two-year mark, contact us urgently — a late filing application requires a clear and documented explanation.

### Q: Can I file my consumer complaint online?

Yes. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 introduced the **e-Daakhil portal** (edaakhil.nic.in) for online filing of consumer complaints. You can file the complaint, pay court fees electronically, upload documents, and track your case from Pune without physically visiting the CDRC on filing day. The Pune District CDRC accepts e-Daakhil filings. Physical filing at the Shivajinagar complex remains an option. For complex complaints with voluminous documents, a lawyer can manage the e-Daakhil process on your behalf.

### Q: My builder gave me possession but the flat has construction defects. Can I claim?

Yes, and this is one of the strongest categories of consumer complaints. Construction defects discovered after possession — seepage, cracking, plumbing failures, substandard materials, or amenities not delivered as promised — are a clear deficiency in service. The limitation period is two years from when you **discovered** the defect (not from the possession date), so you may still be within time even for older projects. Document defects thoroughly with photographs, video, and if possible a written assessment from a structural engineer or licensed plumber. You can claim the cost of repairs, compensation for mental agony and loss of use, and litigation costs.

### Q: The opposite party didn't respond to my consumer complaint. What happens?

If the opposite party fails to file a written version within the allowed time, the CDRC proceeds **ex parte** — it hears and decides the case on the complainant's evidence alone, without the opposite party. Ex parte orders are generally passed in favour of the complainant if the documentary evidence is adequate. The opposite party can later apply to set aside the ex parte order by showing sufficient cause, but consumer commissions are increasingly unsympathetic to large companies that routinely ignore CDRC notices — they face adverse cost orders and adverse findings on conduct.
