# False 498A Case Filed Against You in Pune? Your Immediate Defence Options

A 498A / BNS Section 85 complaint (dowry harassment and cruelty) is one of the most serious and emotionally devastating situations a husband and his family can face. The offence is cognisable and non-bailable — meaning police can arrest without a warrant and bail is not automatic.

And yet, the Supreme Court and Bombay High Court have repeatedly acknowledged that these complaints are frequently filed as a tool in matrimonial disputes rather than genuine allegations of cruelty.

This guide tells you exactly what to do if a false 498A case is filed — or about to be filed — against you in Pune.

---

## What is Section 498A / BNS Section 85?

Section 498A IPC (now Section 85 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, BNS) punishes cruelty by a husband or his relatives towards a wife. Cruelty includes:

1. **Wilful conduct** likely to drive the wife to suicide or cause grave injury to her life, limb or health
2. **Harassment** for unlawful demand for property (dowry)

It is **non-bailable** and carries imprisonment up to 3 years + fine.

The complaint can name the husband, in-laws, and other relatives — all of whom face arrest risk.

---

## Step 1: File for Anticipatory Bail Immediately

**Do not wait for the arrest.**

The moment you learn a 498A FIR has been filed — or that one is likely to be filed — contact a lawyer and file for **anticipatory bail** (Section 482 BNSS) at the Sessions Court or Bombay High Court.

Anticipatory bail protects you and your family from arrest while the case is pending. If granted at the first hearing (which is common in cases where there are obvious signs of falsehood), you receive interim protection until the next date.

**What you need for the application:**
- Copy of the FIR (if already filed)
- ID and address proof for all applicants
- Brief affidavit explaining the circumstances
- Any communication (WhatsApp, email) that contradicts the complaint

---

## Step 2: Assess FIR Quashing at Bombay High Court

Many 498A FIRs can be quashed at the Bombay High Court under Section 528 BNSS if:

- The allegations are vague, general, and don't disclose specific acts of cruelty
- The FIR is filed years after separation — delay defeats the urgency claimed
- The parties have settled or are willing to settle
- The FIR is clearly filed as a pressure tactic in a civil/matrimonial dispute
- The named relatives (parents, siblings) had minimal or no contact with the wife

The Supreme Court in *Rajesh Sharma v. State of UP* (now partially modified) directed a committee mechanism before arrest in 498A cases — creating an additional protection layer.

---

## Step 3: Gather and Preserve Evidence

Begin collecting immediately:

**Evidence of the wife's conduct:**
- WhatsApp messages showing normal relationship before the alleged breakdown
- Social media posts showing the wife was happy (contradicts cruelty allegations)
- Messages or emails from the wife acknowledging she was treated well
- Joint photos and travel records

**Evidence contradicting specific allegations:**
- Bank records showing no dowry demands
- Receipts for gifts given voluntarily (contradicts "demand" narrative)
- Medical records showing no physical injury

**Evidence about named relatives:**
- Geographic distance (parents in another city)
- Lack of contact (call records showing no communication)
- Age or health status (bedridden parent couldn't have committed cruelty)

---

## Step 4: File for Divorce (If the Marriage Has Broken Down)

A false 498A complaint usually signals an irretrievably broken marriage. Filing for divorce — either mutual consent or contested — alongside the criminal defence creates a parallel legal track and opens mediation channels that can resolve both the criminal case and the marriage simultaneously.

A genuine settlement between parties is the strongest ground for quashing a 498A FIR.

---

## Key Supreme Court Judgments Protecting the Accused

**Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)** — Police cannot arrest in 498A cases mechanically. They must first assess if arrest is necessary based on Section 41 BNSS factors. Magistrates must record reasons for authorising detention.

**Social Action Forum v. Union of India (2018)** — Modified the *Rajesh Sharma* guidelines but maintained that a Family Welfare Committee must be consulted before arrest in most cases.

**Preeti Gupta v. State of Jharkhand (2010)** — SC noted the misuse of 498A and the "alarming frequency" of false cases against husbands' families.

## What Happens If You Do Nothing?

- Police will arrest you and your family without warning
- Custody can last weeks before bail is secured
- Even after bail, the case will run for years in Magistrate's Court
- A conviction (even with eventual acquittal) causes permanent reputational damage

Early action — anticipatory bail + FIR quashing — is dramatically more effective than waiting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can my elderly parents also be arrested in a 498A case?**
A: Yes, if they are named in the FIR. However, courts are increasingly reluctant to remand elderly or infirm relatives to custody. Anticipatory bail should be filed for all named accused simultaneously. Bombay High Court has quashed FIRs against in-laws where no specific role was attributed to them.

**Q: How quickly can anticipatory bail be obtained in Pune?**
A: We file within 24–48 hours of being retained. Interim protection is commonly granted at the first hearing. Full hearing typically within 1–2 weeks.

**Q: What is the success rate of FIR quashing in 498A cases?**
A: High, where there are clear grounds — particularly where: (a) allegations are vague, (b) settlement has been reached, or (c) in-laws had no real contact with the wife. We assess your specific FIR before advising on quashing prospects.

**Q: Can the wife add more sections to the FIR later?**
A: A supplementary statement can add new sections. We monitor the investigation and respond to any additions promptly.

**Q: If we settle, does the 498A case get closed?**
A: 498A is non-compoundable as a matter of right — but the Bombay High Court regularly quashes the FIR on the basis of settlement, treating settlement as a ground for quashing under Section 528 BNSS.
