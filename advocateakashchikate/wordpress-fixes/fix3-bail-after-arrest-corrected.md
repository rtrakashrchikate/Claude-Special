# Bail After Arrest in Pune — Types, Process & Timeline (2025)

When someone is arrested in Pune, the immediate question every family has is: how quickly can they come home, and what does it take? This guide explains the types of bail available at Pune courts, the process for each, what a surety has to do, and realistic timelines for different kinds of cases.

---

## Types of Bail

### Bail as of Right (Bailable Offences)
For offences listed as "bailable" in the First Schedule to the BNSS, the arrested person has a legal right to bail. The police station itself can grant bail without any court application. A surety may or may not be required. This covers relatively minor offences — Section 138 NI Act (cheque bounce), minor assault, certain property offences.

### Regular Bail (Non-Bailable Offences) — Section 480 BNSS
For non-bailable offences, bail is not a right — it is at the court's discretion. The accused or their lawyer must file a bail application before the Magistrate or Sessions Court (depending on the offence). The court weighs factors including the nature and gravity of the offence, whether the accused is a flight risk, likelihood of tampering with evidence, and the accused's personal background.

For offences triable by a Magistrate, the bail application is filed before the Magistrate. For offences triable by a Sessions Court — which includes serious matters like Section 498A BNS, cheating, NDPS — the application is typically filed before the Sessions Court, Shivajinagar, Pune.

### Default Bail (Statutory Bail) — Section 187(3) BNSS
This is the most important and most overlooked bail right. Under Section 187(3) BNSS (formerly Section 167(2) CrPC), if police fail to file the chargesheet within the prescribed period while the accused is in custody, the accused has an indefeasible right to bail — regardless of the seriousness of the offence.

**The deadlines are:**
- **90 days** from the date of arrest — for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment of 10 years or more
- **60 days** from the date of arrest — for all other offences

Once either deadline passes without a chargesheet being filed, the accused can apply for bail as a matter of right. The court cannot refuse. However, this right vanishes the instant the chargesheet is filed — even one day late. If 60 or 90 days have passed and no chargesheet has been filed, contact a lawyer immediately.

### Anticipatory Bail — Section 482 BNSS
Pre-arrest protection. Applied for before arrest, where a person has reason to believe they may be arrested. Available at Sessions Court (Shivajinagar, Pune) or the Bombay High Court for more serious matters. If granted, the person cannot be arrested without first being produced before the court — or in some cases, cannot be arrested at all during the period of protection.

### High Court Bail — Section 481 BNSS
When Sessions Court bail is rejected, the next step is a fresh bail application before the Bombay High Court (Principal Seat, Mumbai). This is not simply re-filing the same application — it requires a fresh argument specifically addressing why the Sessions Court's reasons for refusal were wrong or why fresh material justifies a different conclusion.

## The Bail Application Process at Pune Courts

**Step 1 — Brief your lawyer**
Share the FIR copy, the remand order, and all facts of the case. The lawyer identifies which court has jurisdiction and what bail strategy is appropriate.

**Step 2 — Draft the bail application**
The application sets out the accused's background, the nature of the charges, why bail should be granted (flight risk, evidence tampering risk, seriousness of offence — all addressed affirmatively), and proposed surety details.

**Step 3 — Filing and listing**
The application is filed in court and given a number. The court typically lists it for hearing within 1–5 days depending on urgency and the court's docket.

**Step 4 — Hearing**
The accused's lawyer argues for bail; the Public Prosecutor argues against. The judge may hear both sides in 15–20 minutes, or reserve the matter for a full hearing. In urgent matters, same-day hearing can be requested.

**Step 5 — Order**
The court grants or rejects bail. If granted, the order specifies bail amount, surety requirements, and any conditions. If rejected, the reasons for rejection become the basis for a fresh application before the Bombay High Court.

## Surety Requirements at Pune Courts

A surety is a person who provides a guarantee to the court that the accused will attend all future hearings. The surety is not a co-accused — they are a guarantor.

**Who can be a surety:**
- A family member or friend with stable income or property
- The surety should ideally be a Pune/Maharashtra resident
- The surety must not be a proclaimed offender or have a criminal record

**What the surety must bring to court:**
- Personal bond for the amount specified in the bail order
- Proof of property: registered sale deed, 7/12 extract, or property tax receipt
- Identity proof: Aadhaar card, PAN card
- Address proof
- Income proof if required (salary slips, ITR, bank statements)

**Personal Recognizance (PR) bond:** In appropriate cases, a lawyer can apply for PR bail — where the accused is released on their own undertaking, without a separate surety. This is more common in matters where the accused has strong local roots and the charges are not the most serious.

## Realistic Timelines — Pune Courts

| Situation | Typical Timeline |
|-----------|----------------|
| Bailable offence — bail at police station | Same day of arrest |
| Non-bailable offence — bail at Magistrate | 1–3 days |
| Sessions Court bail application (first application) | Filed within 1–2 days; hearing 3–7 days after filing |
| Sessions Court bail rejected — HC application | Filed within 1–2 days of rejection; first HC hearing 4–10 days |
| Interim bail pending full HC hearing | Often at first or second HC hearing |
| Default bail (60/90-day deadline passed) | Application filed same day; Magistrate order usually same or next day |

The most urgent situation is default bail — if the deadline has passed, every day of delay risks the chargesheet being filed and the right being permanently lost.

## Bail Conditions — What to Expect

Courts in Pune typically impose conditions when granting bail in serious cases:

- **Personal bond** — a promise to appear at all future hearings
- **Surety bond** — one or more sureties as described above
- **Passport surrender** — for flight risk cases or serious offences
- **Police station reporting** — weekly or fortnightly
- **Travel restrictions** — not to leave Pune / Maharashtra without court permission
- **Contact restrictions** — not to approach witnesses or the complainant

Violation of bail conditions can result in bail being cancelled and the accused being returned to custody. Follow all conditions precisely.

---

## What Happens If the Court Rejects Bail

A Sessions Court rejection is not the end. The Bombay High Court is an independent forum — a different level of judiciary, with a different judge who looks at the Sessions Court's reasoning and decides whether it was correct. A well-prepared HC application specifically addresses the Sessions Court's grounds for rejection and presents any additional material not before the lower court.

Most criminal defence lawyers in Pune who work at the Bombay High Court would file the HC application within 2–3 days of a Sessions Court rejection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can bail be rejected for a first-time offender?**
Yes — bail is decided based on the nature of the offence, not solely on criminal history. However, being a first-time offender with no prior record is a significant factor in favour of bail, and courts regularly take it into account.

**Does the accused need to be present at the bail hearing?**
In most bail applications, particularly before the Sessions Court, the accused does not need to be present in court for the hearing — the lawyer appears on their behalf. However, if bail is granted, the accused must appear in person to execute the bond.

**Can bail be cancelled after it is granted?**
Yes. The prosecution can apply to cancel bail if the accused violates bail conditions, threatens witnesses, is arrested in another case, or tampers with evidence. This is why strict compliance with all bail conditions is essential.

**My family member is in Yerawada. How long before bail can be arranged?**
For a non-bailable offence at the Sessions Court level: realistically 5–10 days from when the lawyer is briefed to when bail is granted (filing + hearing + order + executing the bond). For default bail where the deadline has passed: often 1–2 days. For a bailable offence: the lawyer can often secure bail at the station or before the Magistrate within 24 hours.

**Can the bail amount be reduced?**
Yes. Under Section 440 CrPC (now corresponding BNSS provision), the accused or a surety can apply for a reduction of the bail amount if it is disproportionate to the accused's financial means. Courts in Pune are generally responsive to such applications when supported by evidence of the accused's financial position.
